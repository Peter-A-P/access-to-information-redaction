Access to information, with every number measured

Hundreds of pages, redacted in an afternoon, and a person still decides every line

When someone asks a public body for its records, staff read every page and decide, line by line, what must be withheld and what must be released. The law turns on context: the requester's own details go back to them, an official signing a letter stays, a neighbour who made a complaint is withheld. Off-the-shelf privacy tools black out every name and number, which answers the request in form and refuses it in substance, so the reading is still done by hand. This tool reads the record the way a reviewer does, proposes every redaction with the section of the law it rests on, and leaves the decision on every page to a person, with a log nobody can quietly edit.

Why it is still done by hand

The same kind of detail, a name or a telephone number, is released on one line and withheld on the next, and nothing in the name says which. What decides it is who the person is in this record. That is the judgement privacy tools do not attempt, so they withhold everything, and a public body that used one would be refusing most of what the law says it must release.

The same words, different answers, because of who they belong to. "Director of Operations" appears twice. On the To: line it is the recipient's title, which says nothing about a person, so it stays. In the body it is the complainant's, and it is withheld with "Regional Director", the respondent's: they are the titles of the two parties to a confidential complaint, and a title released beside a severed name identifies the one person who holds it. The Program Officer who wrote the memorandum is an official doing their job, so their name and work email are released. The requester gets their own name back and nothing else of a file about other people.

When it is unsure, it withholds and asks. The memorandum's recipient, Desmond Okonkwo, is escalated, though a reviewer would release the name: the title sits beside it on the To: line, and the rules do not yet pair the two there. That is the direction the tool errs in on purpose, and the reviewer releases it with one key.

One page, in seven steps

The order is the design. The context is built before anything is decided, the rules decide what has a settled answer, and nothing is released until a person has acted on every page.

  1. 1ReadRecover the layout: letterhead, email headers, signature blocks, even from a scan.
  2. 2FindEvery name, number, address and title that could be personal information.
  3. 3PlaceBuild each person's context: their title, their block, every other mention of them.
  4. 4DecideFixed rules in a fixed order, each tied to a section of the law.
  5. 5AskOptionally, a model on what is left, seeing placeholders, never a name.
  6. 6ReviewA person approves or overrules every proposal, page by page, all of it logged.
  7. 7ReleaseRemove the text under every box, then test that nothing is left.

Step 6 is highlighted because it is the only one that can release anything. How it's built follows a span through all seven.

Does it work?

Two rates, published side by side, because either alone can be gamed

Measured on {{corpus_documents}} invented records holding {{corpus_spans}} labelled spans. The people were cast before the documents were written, so the right answer for every span is known by construction rather than by someone's opinion. Each part starts in plain language; the detail for someone who builds these systems is folded under For the technical reader.

Withholding what must go, releasing what must stay

A leak is a third party's information released. Over-redaction is something the requester was entitled to, withheld. A tool can drive the first to zero by withholding everything, and the blunt tool nearly does; the price is the second number, which is the one nobody publishes. Lower and further left is better on both.

{{rules_leak}}of what must be withheld, released ({{rules_leak_ci}}), against {{blunt_leak}} for the blunt tool
{{rules_esc}}of spans sent to a person as undecidable ({{rules_esc_ci}}); every one is withheld until they say otherwise
{{recall}}of labelled spans found by the detector at all; a span never found cannot be decided

The leak rate is above the blunt tool's, and it is meant to be visible. The gap, {{rules_leak}} against {{blunt_leak}}, is what releasing anything at all costs. A tool that never released a span would have the lowest leak rate on the page and no reason to exist. A person reviews every proposal precisely because of that gap, and the study measures what is left after they do.

The context is the whole argument

A person's capacity is almost never in the sentence that names them. It is three inches lower, in the signature block, or at the top, on the letterhead. So for every span the tool builds a context record: the kind of block it sits in, the heading above it, whether a title is paired with this person anywhere in the record, whether they are on a public register, every other mention of the same person, and whether they are the requester. Blank that record and keep the same rules, and over-redaction more than doubles.

What each kind of release costs. Officials are most of what must be released, so theirs is the figure to watch, and most of it is escalation rather than a wrong answer: a name or a work address the record never pairs with a title, which the tool will not guess at. Where every leak comes from

A leak can only happen four ways: the detector never found the span; it found it and called it a place or an organisation, which is not personal information; the box was too short; or the decision was wrong. Counting them separately says which part to fix, and a two-way split once pointed a fix at the wrong module.

The rules themselves leak nothing. Of the rules-only row's {{rules_leaks}} leaks, {{rules_never_found}} were never found, {{rules_mislabelled}} were found and typed as a place or an organisation, {{rules_box_short}} left part of a name showing, and {{rules_decision_wrong}} were a wrong decision. The weak part is detection, and that is where the work goes.

What that adds up to

Federal institutions processed {{federal_pages}} pages of access-to-information requests in {{federal_pages_year}}, by the Treasury Board's statistical report (Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada excluded, as the report excludes it). At the {{secs_saved}} seconds a page the study measured, that is about {{hours_saved}} hours of review saved a year, or {{people_saved}} people reviewing full time ({{hours_per_year}} hours a year each). At the top-step salary of a PM-04 analyst, the level federal ATIP analysts are most often classified at, ${{analyst_salary}} in the Program and Administrative Services agreement (effective June 2024), that time is worth about ${{dollars_saved}} million a year, before benefits or overhead, so the real cost is higher.

This is arithmetic on a measurement, not a measurement. The seconds come from one-page invented records, and a real page can be denser or emptier; it counts only the reading and deciding, not finding the records, consulting other bodies or writing the response. Read it as the scale of the work the tool is aimed at.

Faster and more accurate, which is the combination that matters. By hand, the misses were the kind people make: a first name standing alone later in the text, the second appearance of a full name, a selection one character short of an email address. With proposals, the misses were the tool's own boxes, approved as drawn. Both kinds are why the screen now withholds every other occurrence of a value the moment a reviewer withholds one.

For the technical reader: what the study can and cannot say
  • {{reviewers_word_cap}} people, the first with eight years in access-to-information work. Every reviewer did the proposals half second, so some of the gain is familiarity with the templates; the interval resamples records, not people.
  • The proposals are frozen as they stood for the first reviewer, so every later reviewer saw the same tool and the arms stay comparable as the tool improves.
  • The reviewers changed the labels twice, and every table carries both rulings: a family word that points at a third party is withheld with the name, and the parties to a confidential workplace complaint are withheld with their titles. Where a reviewer departed from a label and the label stood, it counts against the reviewer.
  • Seconds are the server's own, from serving a page to its completion row in the log. results/reviewer.md and docs/reviewer-study.md.

A black box is not a redaction

The most common redaction failure in the news is a black rectangle drawn over text that is still in the file, one copy and paste from public. Every export here removes the text under each box, and then a test tries to get it back two ways: from the PDF's text layer, and by rendering the page to an image and reading it with OCR.

EveryPDF this site serves has its text layer tested the same way before it is sent, and is refused if anything is found

Try it: review a record, release it, download the PDF and select the text under a box. There is nothing there to select.

Scanned paper

Many records arrive as scans. The same {{scan_documents}} records were printed to PDF, degraded the way a scanner, a photocopier and a fax degrade a page, and read back with Tesseract. What a good scan costs is not the characters; it is the layout. A scan has no signature block in it, only lines of text, so the tool reads the blocks back from the words a signature and an email header are made of.

A scanner or a photocopier costs about a point; a fax costs the record. Reading the layout back takes an office scanner's over-redaction from {{scan_scanner_flat_over}} to {{scan_scanner_over}}, against {{scan_native_over}} on the native file. At {{scan_fax_cer}} of characters misread, a fax destroys the values before any detector sees them, and the leak rate is {{scan_fax_leak}}. Fax-quality records need a person, and the tool's number says so.

Real released records

Invented records have exact labels and template-shaped text. Published federal releases are the opposite: real, untidy, and everything still visible was judged releasable by a person at Library and Archives Canada. So the question asked of them is one-sided but honest: of what a person released, how much would this tool have withheld? {{rel_packages_total}} packages, {{rel_pages_total}} pages, in three sets, the last two chosen by a rule written before any was downloaded.

This is the tool's weakest result, and it is published as such. On the Defence records it learned from, teaching it military ranks and reading layout back took it from withholding everything to {{rel_defence_now}}. On records it never saw, it still withholds {{rel_heldout_now}} and {{rel_fresh_now}}: heads of state it has no register for, family words in century-old immigration files that the archive opened because everyone in them was born more than 110 years ago. The gain did not travel, and the page says so rather than quoting only the first set.

For the technical reader: why a leak rate cannot be reported here
  • One-sided by construction. What sat under the original black boxes was removed before publication, so there is no must-withhold denominator. The scoring code raises rather than return a leak rate on this track.
  • A floor, not a forecast. These are OCR text layers of scanned forms with no layout and no configured requester, so every effect pushes towards withholding.
  • The archive's own age rule (release once 110 years have passed since birth, Privacy Regulations s. 6(c)) was built and registered before the third set was opened; no page in it was old enough for the rule to read, so it decided nothing there, and that is reported too.
  • releases.md, releases-heldout.md, releases-fresh.md, and the selection rules in docs/data.md.

The language model, shown the page

The rules decide what has a settled answer and escalate the rest. A language model is asked about what is left, through the portfolio's own AI gateway. It sees the whole page with every name, contact and identifier replaced by a typed placeholder, the same one wherever that person appears, so it can tell who signed and who wrote to whom without seeing a single name. Its answers are taken at a confidence threshold chosen on separate records.

{{full_over}}over-redaction with the model deciding what the rules could not, against {{rules_over}} with the rules alone
{{model_extra_leaks}}of the model's decisions released a third party's details that had to be withheld: a leak rate of {{full_leak}} against {{rules_leak}}
{{model_cost_page}}a page through the gateway ({{model_cost_page_cad}}), with {{model_name}}
{{gate_verdict}}project 03's release gate: {{gate_worse}} documents worse with the model, {{gate_better}} better

A stronger model was not the answer; showing the model the page was. Given one sentence around each name, even the largest model available said "cannot tell" on hundreds of spans, and said why: the sentence did not show who the person was. Given the page, the reviewers' category guide and which addresses are the public body's own, the smallest one decides nearly all of it. The portfolio's release gate, asked with the document as the unit, passes it: {{gate_rules}} of documents leak-free with the rules alone, {{gate_model}} with the model, inside its three-point margin. Which to run is still a public body's decision, and the tool runs the rules alone unless told otherwise.

For the technical reader: what weakens the result
  • Some of what the model knows came from this corpus's errors. Given the page, it first released employee numbers and an outside physician's practice address. Both rulings existed; neither was in the prompt. They are now, and the corpus that exposed them is the one scored here.
  • On real released records the gain is smaller. On packages nothing here was tuned on, the share of possibly personal spans withheld falls from 100 percent to 84.5 with the model.
  • Without the rules the model leaks {{model_only_leak}} against {{full_leak}} with them, and withholds far more. It reads a person where the detector saw a place, which the rules cannot; the rules settle two thirds of the spans for nothing.
  • No raw entity leaves. A test asserts that no detected value appears in any outbound request, page included; the gateway records the data class of every call. {{model_asks}} questions were asked for the published tables, and answers are cached so the tables reproduce for nothing.

The law, as data

Every proposal names a category, and every category maps to the sections of the legislation it rests on, in two jurisdictions. The mapping is a data file, not code, so a public body can read it, argue with it and change it. Each of the {{provisions}} provisions carries the quoted text, the date it was checked and the address it was checked against, and the file refuses one that has none.

{{taxonomy_rows}}
CategoryCanada (federal)Newfoundland and Labrador

ATIA is the Access to Information Act; ATIPPA is Newfoundland and Labrador's Access to Information and Protection of Privacy Act, 2015. Where the two disagree, as on what is already public, the provincial file escalates rather than releases. The tool decides only personal information: advice, legal privilege, law enforcement and business interests are flagged for the reviewer with the provision and never decided.

What did not work

Looking officials up in the staff directory by name. Most of the over-redaction of officials is a name the record never pairs with a title, and the obvious remedy is to look the name up. Measured, it multiplied the leak rate by {{dir_leak_factor_word}}, from {{dir_leak_none}} to {{dir_leak_names}}. Some of that is the small invented name pool, but {{dir_listed_leaks}} of the leaks were the listed employee themselves: an employee who files a grievance is in the directory and is a third party in their own complaint. A directory is evidence about a string; the decision turns on the sentence. Only the work email address survived, and it is now the only thing a directory is matched on.

What this does not do

It does not release anything

Every page needs a person's explicit action, and the export asks the audit log, which refuses while any page is undecided.

It decides personal information only

Advice, legal privilege, law enforcement and business interests are flagged with the provision and left to the reviewer.

It is not legal advice

The categories map to two statutes and are configurable. A public body applies its own interpretation.

The corpus is invented

Exact labels need people cast before the documents are written. Real releases, above, are where that is checked, and they are its weakest result.

It does not read handwriting

The scanned track covers typed pages. A fax-quality page loses most of its values before detection.

The study is three people

Each did both halves, proposals second, so familiarity is part of the gain. The interval resamples records, not reviewers.

For the technical reader: the rules the code enforces on itself
  • No path to release without a person. The export asks the session's release gate, which reads the audit log and raises while any page lacks a completion row.
  • Withhold and escalate when unsure. An undecided span is withheld and flagged; no rule releases a span because nothing matched.
  • The audit log is append-only. A reviewer who changes their mind adds a row; each row's hash covers the one before it, and the log stores a hash of the withheld text rather than a second copy of it.
  • Every model call goes through the gateway as personal data, and a test asserts that no raw detected value appears in an outbound request.
  • Every reported number carries an interval and its track, and every table is written by the command that measured it. This page reads those tables, so it cannot disagree with them.

How this was built

Design, methodology, evaluation choices and judgement are Peter Parker's, from eight years inside a public body that answers access-to-information requests, where the question on this page is asked about every page released. AI coding assistants (Claude Code) were used for implementation and drafting, the way a senior engineer uses them in 2026. Every number on this page is reproducible from the repository.

Glossary
Access-to-information request
A request by anyone for a public body's records, which the body must answer, withholding only what the law allows.
Confidence interval
The range a measured rate would plausibly fall in if the measurement were repeated. A bare number without one is treated as a defect here.
Context record
What the tool knows about one span before deciding it: its block, its heading, the person's title anywhere in the record, other mentions, a register entry, and whether they are the requester.
Escalation
A proposal to withhold and ask a person, because the record does not say enough to decide.
Leak rate
Of the details that must be withheld, the share released.
Over-redaction
Of the details that must be released, the share withheld. The number blunt tools never publish.
Severance
One piece of a record withheld under a provision, listed in the schedule sent to the requester.
Span
One stretch of text the tool found and decided: a name, a number, an address, a title.
