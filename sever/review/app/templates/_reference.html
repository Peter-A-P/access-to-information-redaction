{# The rules the study's records were labelled under, in the words a reviewer needs. Shown on the welcome page and folded away on every record, in both arms, so that neither arm is working from facts the other does not have. #}
  • Release the requester's own information. Their name, address, telephone, date of birth, health and file numbers. The requester is named at the top of every record.
  • Release officials acting in their official capacity: the name, job title and business contact details of public servants doing their jobs. Withhold an official's employee number and anything personal about them, such as a home address or a health number.
  • Release what is already public: a name printed on the record's own letterhead, and the people on the public register below.
  • Release information about someone dead more than twenty years.
  • Release what is not personal information: organisations, places that are not someone's address, dates, and job titles on their own.
  • Withhold everything personal about anyone else: their names (including a first name used on its own), addresses, telephone numbers, email addresses, identifiers and dates of birth. That includes outside professionals, such as a physician writing about a client, and family members.
  • When you cannot tell whether someone is an official or a third party, withhold it. In the proposals arm, Escalate does that.
